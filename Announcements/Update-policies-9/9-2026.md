## Risk Classification Note (Việt Nam–oriented)

**Document type:** Policy orientation (not a legal opinion)  
**Organization:** Adaptive Intelligence Circle (AIC)  
**Related:** Ethical Kernel · Human Meaning Network (HMN) · Governance evaluate · Third Path  
**Status:** Pre-Covenant  
**Version:** 1.0 · September 2026  

#### 1. Purpose

This note explains how Adaptive Intelligence Circle (AIC) treats **risk classification** in light of Vietnam’s AI regulatory framework, including:

- Law on Artificial Intelligence No. 134/2025/QH15 (effective 1 March 2026);
- Decree No. 142/2026/ND-CP guiding implementation;
- Decision No. 33/2026/QĐ-TTg (list of high-risk AI systems; relevant dates from mid-August 2026 onward as published by the State).

This document is **internal/public protocol policy**. It is **not**:

- a certificate of compliance;
- a substitute for advice from licensed counsel;
- a claim that AIC repositories, TestNet, or documentation are exempt from Vietnamese law when a concrete AI system is provided or deployed in Vietnam.

**Independence ≠ immunity.** Partner, fiscal host, or international contact ≠ regulator ≠ shield.

#### 2. Scope

| In scope | Out of scope |
|----------|----------------|
| Orientation for AIC maintainers, deployers of AIC-derived systems, and public observers | Binding determination of risk level for every fork or third-party product |
| Mapping AIC design (human gate, audit, refusal) to risk *thinking* | Automatic registration, notification, or conformity filing on behalf of third parties |
| Clear separation: **protocol research** vs **operational AI system** | Defense, security, or cipher systems excluded by law (handled only by competent authorities) |

Anyone who **packages, hosts, or offers** an AI system using AIC components for users in Vietnam remains responsible for their own classification, dossiers, and notifications under applicable law.

#### 3. Principles (AIC)

1. **Law first.** Where Vietnamese law requires classification, notification, labeling, human oversight, or other duties, those duties apply to the obligated party. Protocol philosophy does not override statute.
2. **Honest role separation.** Open repositories, whitepapers, and parallel TestNets are not the same object as a production service that makes or supports decisions affecting persons, rights, or public interests.
3. **Human Meaning Network (HMN).** Meaning-critical and high-impact acts should remain **human-gated** (NeedHuman / accept) by design—not as a slogan after deployment.
4. **Fail-closed ethics.** Ethical Kernel constraints and audit trails are intended to make “strip ethics under pressure” harder—not to replace regulatory risk classes.
5. **Under-claim.** AIC does not declare global Mainnet/Covenant completion, UN endorsement, or blanket “low-risk” status for all uses of its code.

#### 4. Regulatory orientation (Việt Nam)

###### 4.1 Risk tiers (framework-level)

Vietnam’s framework classifies AI systems in tiers commonly described as **high-risk**, **medium-risk**, and **low-risk** (exact legal tests are those in the Law, Decree, and implementing instruments—not this note).

High-risk orientation in public materials emphasizes systems that may cause **significant harm** to life, health, lawful rights and interests, national interests, public interests, or national security, together with sector, scale, automation, and decision role.

###### 4.2 Self-classification and process

Providers (and other obligated roles as defined by law) should expect to:

- **self-classify** before putting a system into use where required;
- prepare and retain a **classification dossier** for medium- and high-risk systems as required;
- **notify** the competent authority (e.g. via the national AI single-window / mechanisms under the Ministry of Science and Technology) when the law so requires;
- respect **lists and criteria** issued by the Prime Minister / Government (including Decision 33/2026/QĐ-TTg and successor instruments);
- observe **transition / grace periods** applicable to systems already in operation before relevant effective dates.

AIC maintainers do **not** file a single “org-wide” classification that covers every fork, demo, or external deployment.

###### 4.3 Human oversight

Public implementing guidance has associated **independent, substantive human review before a decision takes effect** with important legal consequences for how some systems are treated.

AIC’s governance pipeline (**Allow / Deny / NeedHuman**) and HMN seats are designed so that high-impact actions can be required to stop for human acceptance.  

**Design alignment is not automatic legal reclassification.** Only the facts of a specific deployment (who decides, whether human review is real, sector, scale) matter under the law.

#### 5. How to classify *AIC-related* artifacts (practical)

###### 5.1 Default reading of core AIC surfaces

| Artifact | Typical reading (orientation only) | Notes |
|----------|--------------------------------------|--------|
| Public source code, specs, whitepaper | Research / documentation infrastructure | Still not a license to deploy unlawfully |
| Local TestNet / CI / lab evaluate | Engineering test environment | If exposed as a public decision service, reassess |
| Public demo chatbot or agent API | Likely an **AI system** under a broad statutory definition | Apply disclosure + classify by use case |
| Production service using AIC kernel for credit, hiring, health, education, public admin, etc. | May fall into **medium- or high-risk** per list/criteria | Dossier + notification + human oversight as required |
| Transparency / audit logs only | Support control, not a substitute for classification | Keep integrity of logs |

###### 5.2 Questions deployers must answer (before production)

1. Who is the **provider** / **deployer** under Vietnamese law for this instance?  
2. What **outputs** affect persons, rights, safety, or public functions?  
3. Is the system on or analogous to the **high-risk list** / criteria in force?  
4. Is there **substantive human review** before effects take place—or only cosmetic confirmation?  
5. Are **transparency** duties triggered (user informed they interact with AI; synthetic content labeling where required)?  
6. Has the team recorded a **written self-classification** and retention path for evidence?

If answers are unclear, treat the system as **higher caution** until clarified with competent guidance or counsel—not as “AIC open source ⇒ low risk.”

#### 6. AIC internal obligations (protocol policy)

For official AIC-operated public endpoints (if any):

1. Publish an **AI interaction disclosure** (users know they interact with an AI system).  
2. Do not offer production decision-making in listed high-sensitivity domains without a completed classification path and human-gate design.  
3. Prefer **NeedHuman** for constitutional, mass-impact, or rights-impacting actions in governance code.  
4. Record evaluate/audit events; do not disable Ethical Kernel for convenience.  
5. Update this note when major implementing instruments change—without rewriting history of prior TestNet phases.

For **community forks**: the fork operator owns compliance. AIC may refuse support for deployments that claim AIC endorsement while ignoring applicable law.

#### 7. Prohibited claims (narrative discipline)

AIC personnel and official channels shall **not** state or imply that:

- AIC is approved, certified, or endorsed by the State of Vietnam solely because it is open source or non-profit;
- informal international meetings create exemption from the AI Law or Decree;
- “Third Path” or “Zero Donation” replaces risk classification;
- pre-Covenant TestNet equals a finished, law-cleared production system for all sectors.

#### 8. Relationship to Third Path

Third Path means AIC declines capture by pure commercial rule-power **and** declines becoming a silent instrument of any single geopolitical bloc.  

It does **not** mean operating outside Vietnamese law.  
Lawful presence and honest classification are part of durable independence—not a concession of protocol ownership to any private lab consortium.
