# AIC Foundation Model

**Document type:** Institutional architecture  
**Status:** Canonical reference for AIC-Legal  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-31  

---

## 1. Overview

Adaptive Intelligence Circle (AIC) operates under a **two-layer institutional model**. Both layers share a common origin and purpose, but serve distinct functions and carry different responsibilities.

| Layer | Name | Primary Function | Character |
|-------|------|------------------|-----------|
| Layer 1 | **AIC** | Technical development, community coordination, reference implementations | Protocol / community layer |
| Layer 2 | **AIC Foundation** | Stewardship, legal personality, long-term continuity, external relations | Institutional / stewardship layer |

The two layers are not separate organisations in spirit. They are differentiated expressions of the same initiative. AIC is the originating whole; the Foundation is the formal institutional instrument created to support its continuity, legitimacy, and external engagement.

---

## 2. Shared Origin and Principle

Both layers derive from the same foundational commitments:

- **Ethical-from-Kernel** — ethical constraints are treated as architectural, not as optional policy layers.
- **Third Path Absolute** — independence from capture by either pure market logic or pure state logic.
- **Zero Donation / non-extractive orientation** — the initiative is not structured for private profit extraction.
- **Long-horizon responsibility** — decisions are evaluated against multi-decade consequences, not short-term metrics.
- **Global South origin with international operational optionality** — conceptual and intellectual roots in Vietnam / Global South, with legal and deployment flexibility through neutral or aligned jurisdictions (notably EU fiscal hosting arrangements).

These principles apply to both the technical-community layer and the institutional layer. The Foundation does not dilute or override them; it exists to protect and operationalise them over time.

---

## 3. Layer 1 — AIC (Technical + Community)

**Role:** The living technical and social body of the initiative.

**Responsibilities include:**
- Development and maintenance of reference implementations (including Parallel TestNet phases and subsequent networks).
- Open collaboration, contribution processes, and community coordination.
- Publication of technical specifications, research, and public documentation.
- Operation of public communication and collaboration channels.

**Current public presence (non-exhaustive):**  
GitHub, GitLab, Codeberg, Open Collective, DGPA submission materials, Lounge.ai, Reddit, Medium, Zenodo, YouTube, LinkedIn, RocketReach, Matrix, Signal, Substack, Midnight Forum, and related public artefacts.

**Character:**  
This layer is intentionally lightweight, transparent, and contribution-oriented. It prioritises technical integrity, documentation quality, and the ability of independent parties to inspect, run, and build upon the work.

---

## 4. Layer 2 — AIC Foundation (Institutional Stewardship)

**Role:** The formal institutional instrument that provides legal personality, continuity, and external interface.

**Responsibilities include:**
- Holding and stewarding the long-term purpose of AIC.
- Providing a stable legal and fiscal host arrangement (currently via established non-profit / fiscal-host infrastructure in the EU context).
- Managing formal external relations (standards bodies, public-interest alliances, institutional partners) in a manner consistent with Third Path principles.
- Safeguarding trademarks, key artefacts, and institutional memory where appropriate.
- Ensuring that growth in visibility or resources does not compromise the core constraints (Ethical-from-Kernel, non-extractive orientation, independence).

**Character:**  
The Foundation is deliberately designed as a **stewardship vehicle**, not as a command-and-control centre. It exists to reduce single-person dependency over time and to give the initiative a durable public face that can interact with formal systems without forcing the technical layer into premature centralisation.

## 5. Relationship Between the Two Layers

```
                    AIC (Originating Whole)
                            │
            ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
            │                               │
   Layer 1: AIC                    Layer 2: AIC Foundation
   (Technical + Community)         (Institutional Stewardship)
            │                               │
            └───────────────┬───────────────┘
                            │
                   Shared principles & purpose
```

Key properties of the relationship:

1. **Common mother** — Both layers are expressions of AIC. Neither is an independent commercial or political project.
2. **Functional differentiation** — Technical and community work remains primarily in Layer 1. Formal legal personality, fiscal arrangements, and long-term stewardship sit in Layer 2.
3. **Non-subordination of principles** — The Foundation does not have authority to override Ethical-from-Kernel, Third Path, or Zero Donation commitments.
4. **Progressive decentralisation intent** — The existence of the Foundation is a step toward reducing key-person risk. Over time, stewardship functions are expected to become more distributed while remaining bound by the same constitutional principles.
5. **Visual and nominal distinction** — AIC maintains two logos: one associated with the technical/community layer and one associated with the Foundation layer. Both remain recognisably part of the same family.

## 6. Governance Intent

The model is designed to avoid two common failure modes:

- **Capture by growth logic** — where institutional or financial success gradually rewrites the original purpose.
- **Capture by personality** — where the initiative remains permanently dependent on a single founder or small closed group.

Accordingly:

- Major directional decisions should remain referable to the published principles and Whitepaper.
- The Foundation’s role is custodial and enabling, not proprietary.
- Technical direction and community processes retain substantial autonomy within the shared ethical and architectural constraints.
- Any future evolution of governance (additional stewards, formal boards, or distributed mechanisms) must preserve the non-extractive and Third Path character of the whole.

---

## 7. Public Presence and Risk Dispersion

AIC’s appearance across multiple platforms is intentional. It reflects a strategy of **dispersed presence**:

- No single platform is treated as the sole source of truth or the sole channel of legitimacy.
- Technical artefacts, institutional records, long-form writing, and community spaces are distributed so that the initiative is harder to erase, capture, or misrepresent through the failure or policy change of any one venue.
- The Foundation layer provides a stable institutional reference point that can outlast individual platform accounts or temporary communication channels.
