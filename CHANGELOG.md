## [2026-11] - Post-DPGA Orientation Update

### Added

- Added Governance Impact Assessment concept to support review of governance consequences before introducing policy tooling.
- Added Institutional Risk categories:
  - Founder Dependency
  - Knowledge Concentration
  - Platform Dependency
  - Regulatory Dependency
  - Funding Dependency
  - Jurisdiction Dependency
- Added Stewardship-oriented review checklist.
- Added Public Interest Review checklist.
- Added Human Agency review checklist to preserve meaningful human oversight.
- Added Assumption Register framework for documenting policy assumptions and limitations.
- Added Falsifiable Policy Claims concept for testing policy-related hypotheses.
- Added DPI/DPG alignment orientation notes.

### Documentation

- Expanded documentation around institutional resilience.
- Expanded documentation around governance continuity.
- Expanded limitations regarding automated interpretations of regulatory frameworks.
- Clarified distinction between policy orientation and legal compliance.
- Clarified relationship between governance guidance and contributor judgment.

### Governance

- Reinforced "entity ≠ immunity" principle.
- Reinforced "under-claim" principle across policy artifacts.
- Reinforced requirement for human review when uncertainty exists.
- Improved alignment with long-term stewardship and succession planning concepts.

### DPI / Public Interest

- Improved documentation supporting public-interest infrastructure orientation.
- Improved compatibility with Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) discussions without claiming DPI status.
- Added guidance encouraging transparency, accountability and explainability analysis.

### Institutional Learning

Key observations collected during 2026:

- Infrastructure can outgrow founders.
- Governance failures often emerge before technical failures.
- Institutional memory is a strategic asset.
- Human agency remains essential in AI-assisted environments.
- Public-interest infrastructure requires both technical and governance disciplines.
- Independence should not imply isolation.
- Network trust is often more durable than short-term visibility.
